- ch 2 [low] Section 1 (identity axiom) and Quiz Q1 answer: The identity axiom is written as 'an element e∈G satisfies e∗a = a∗e = a' without the quantifier 'for every a∈G'. As written it only asserts equality for one unnamed a, which is weaker than the standard axiom; a student copying this into an exam answer loses the meaning that e works for all elements.
- ch 2 [low] Worked example 1, step 7: The case analysis is compressed into one sentence: 'containing 2 but not 1 gives even residues; containing 4 gives {0,4}'. Read out of order, 'containing 4 gives {0,4}' is false, because a subgroup containing both 2 and 4 is {0,2,4,6}. The order of the cases carries the logic, and a student skimming may miss it.
- ch 3 [high] Section 3 (Cayley's theorem proof): The inverse step is printed without superscripts: 'its inverse is La-1, because La-1(La(g))=a-1ag=g'. Read literally, a-1ag means a − 1·a·g, which is not g, so the proof step looks false and the student cannot see why L_a is a bijection.
- ch 3 [low] Section 1: Powers are printed without superscripts in the text: '⟨a⟩={ak : k∈Z}', 'a subgroup of ⟨a⟩ is ⟨am⟩', and 'the smallest positive integer n with an=e'. A student can read 'ak' as a times k, which is a different thing.
- ch 3 [low] Section 1 formula and definition of ord(a): The definition ord(a) = min{n ≥ 1 : a<sup>n</sup> = e} is undefined when no such n exists, that is, for elements of infinite order such as 1 in Z. The chapter never says what ord(a) means then.
