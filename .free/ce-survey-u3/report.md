## Reviewer notes (after any revision)

- ch 1 [low] Lead: "A levelling survey finds heights by measuring vertical distances from one fixed zero surface" suggests the surveyor measures directly from the datum, such as MSL. A levelling instrument only measures height differences between nearby points; the datum enters through the known benchmark and the chain of backsights and foresights. Students may carry the wrong picture into the booking chapter.
- ch 1 [low] Section 3, the formula box: RL(new) = RL(known) + BS − FS is stated without saying that the BS and the FS must come from the same instrument setup. Students often combine a BS from one setup with an FS from another and get a wrong RL.
- ch 1 [low] Figure 'One step of the height chain', node notes: The arithmetic notes carry no units: '50.00 + 2.50 = 52.50' and '52.50 − 1.80 = 50.70'. Every other number in the chapter is in metres, so these can be read as bare numbers.
- ch 3 [low] Quiz Q7: The question says an intermediate sight has already been taken at chainage 40 m, but the answer tells the student to 'read it as a foresight'. The reading is already in the book; what changes is how it is booked and that the staff must not be lifted. A student may think a second, different reading has to be taken at the same point.
- ch 3 [low] Section 1, HI formula: The formula is written HI = RL_BM + BS, but the symbol is defined as 'the reduced level of the backsight point', which is not always the benchmark on later setups. The name BM and the definition do not match, and a student may think HI can only be computed from a benchmark.
- ch 4 [low] Section 1, bullets on the foreseeing of a change point, and Worked example 1 / Figure: The book says the FS station is marked as a change point, and the bullet list tells the student to 'mark that station as a change point'. In the worked example and the figure, B is an FS taken with no later backsight, so no instrument move follows; calling B a change point contradicts the definition given in Section 3 ('a station read as an FS, then as a new BS').
- ch 4 [low] Section 2, first paragraph: 'If the next reading is smaller, the ground rose: the line of sight sits lower above the ground' is easily misread, because the line of sight itself does not move during a set-up. What is smaller is the height of that line above the staff zero.
- ch 4 [low] Section 2 (and Quiz Q8): 'This method shows the exact line where a wrong entry was made' overstates what the check does. The arithmetic checks catch addition and reduction slips and locate the page line where the imbalance first appears; they cannot detect a wrongly read or wrongly booked staff reading, because any set of readings will still balance.
- ch 4 [low] Lead paragraph: The lead promises 'two arithmetic checks', but the chapter teaches a single three-part check line (ΣBS − ΣFS = ΣRise − ΣFall = last RL − first RL) applied to the same book.
- ch 5 [high] Lead: The sentence 'Only a few universities teach this advanced correction' is factually wrong. Curvature and refraction correction, and reciprocal levelling, are standard topics in every surveying syllabus and every standard textbook. A student reading this could wrongly skip the topic as optional.
- ch 5 [low] Section 1, first sentence: 'Two points A and B, distance d apart, share a level line' reads as if A and B are the same point or an undefined line. The student cannot tell that the derivation places both stations on the same level surface.
- ch 5 [low] Section 2, residual paragraph: 'A value near 0.0673 d² is expected; a small leftover points to collimation error' is unclear — a reader can take 'small leftover' to mean a small value of ε itself, when the point is the excess over 0.0673 d².
- ch 6 [low] Section 2, spacing rule paragraph: The sentence 'doubling the slope halves the spacing' is false if slope means the angle θ, because HE depends on tan θ, not on θ. Doubling θ does not halve HE (at 20° HE is 2.75 times CI; at 40° it is 1.19 times CI).
- ch 6 [low] Quiz Q2, answer: 'As the valley sides rise upstream' is muddled; it is the valley bed (the line of the stream) that rises upstream, not the sides.
- ch 7 [low] Section 2 and Worked example 1, steps 2 and 6: The chapter says capacity is measured from the basin floor, but the example treats capacity at the lowest contour (100 m) as zero. Volume lying between the basin floor and the 100 m contour is silently dropped, so 'capacity at 106 m = 98 000 m³' and 'capacity at 102 m = 16 000 m³' are not really measured from the floor, and a careful student will see the contradiction.
