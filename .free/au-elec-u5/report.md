## Reviewer notes (after any revision)

- ch 1 [low] Section 3, torque converter paragraph: The lock-up clutch is called 'a plain on/off clutch'. Most production TCUs now apply it with controlled slip (partial lock-up) to smooth torsional vibration, so the flat statement can mislead a student and would lose marks if repeated in an exam.
- ch 1 [low] Section 2, 'Torque stage' bullet: 'Too early and the engine flares; too late and the car jerks' does not say which event is early or late, so a student cannot tell whether the off-going clutch released too early or the on-coming clutch applied too late.
- ch 1 [low] Section 2, 'Learning' bullet: 'If it is longer than target, the TCU lengthens the fill pulse or raises pressure' is stated as a general rule. A long shift caused by low pressure is made worse by a longer fill pulse, and the quiz Q6 answer rightly lengthens the fill pulse only for flare, not for every slow shift. The two statements read as contradictory.
- ch 1 [low] Figure, node 'Solenoid valves': The note 'set line pressure' credits all solenoid valves with setting line pressure. Only the pressure control (variable-force) solenoid trims line pressure; the shift solenoids direct oil to the clutch pistons. The figure also shows only the output shaft sensor, while Section 2 uses input shaft speed as well.
- ch 1 [low] Lead and Quiz Q1: The lead says the TCU reads throttle position, vehicle speed and engine speed, but Q1 asks for the two inputs to the shift map and marks only throttle and vehicle speed. A student may wonder why engine speed is listed and then not accepted.
- ch 1 [low] Quiz Q3 answer: The second sentence, 'The gap also lets the schedule favour economy near the boundary', is vague and not explained anywhere in the chapter.
- ch 2 [low] Worked example 1, step 6 and answer: Part (f) asks for the deceleration that removes the 5 m/s closing speed over the 12 s time-to-zero-gap. The arithmetic 5 ÷ 12 = 0.42 m/s² is right, but the answer reads as if 0.42 m/s² were the deceleration the ACC must produce. It is only the deceleration that brings the closing speed to zero at 12 s, when 30 m of gap are left. A student can wrongly conclude this is the minimum needed to stay safe. The minimum relative deceleration to stop the gap reaching zero is a = closing² ÷ (2d) = 5² ÷ (2 × 60) ≈ 0.21 m/s². To fall back to the 50 m target gap, the host must lose 5 m/s over 10 m, i.e. 5² ÷ (2 × 10) = 1.25 m/s².
- ch 2 [low] Section 1, COAST sentence: 'COAST lowers the stored speed without using the pedal' is vague. On most cars COAST closes the throttle and lets the car slow while the button is held; the lower speed is stored as the new v_set only when the button is released. As written, a student may think COAST directly edits v_set without any vehicle deceleration.
- ch 2 [low] Section 1, formula definition: Kp and Ki are named but no units are given, while e is in m/s and u in %. A student cannot check the units of each term.
- ch 2 [low] Section 2, target selection sentence: 'picks the nearest car in the host lane with the smallest time gap' reads like two separate tests. For one host speed the nearest car in the lane is the smallest time gap, so the phrasing can confuse.
- ch 3 [low] Section 1, first paragraph: "Hydraulic power steering always drives a pump from the engine belt" is too absolute. Electro-hydraulic power steering (EHPS) exists on many cars: it keeps the hydraulic rack but drives the pump with an electric motor, not the engine belt. The word "always" makes a student mark a true statement false.
- ch 3 [low] Figure caption and figure spec: The caption and the aria text say the reduction gear turns motor torque into "assist torque at the rack", but the formula and Section 1 define assist torque at the pinion. The node note under "Rack assist" also says "torque at pinion", so the figure contradicts itself and the text.
- ch 3 [low] Section 2, second paragraph: "How far the wheel is turned from straight ahead" is ambiguous: it can be read as the road wheel angle. The steering angle sensor measures the steering-wheel rotation (several turns lock to lock), which is then converted to road-wheel angle.
- ch 3 [low] Exam traps, third bullet: The trap says "students often divide by N when they should multiply", but the worked example itself divides 8 N·m by 10 to get motor torque. Without saying which direction is which, a student may think the example breaks the rule.
- ch 4 [low] Worked example 1, step 2: λ = 0.10 sits exactly on the lower edge of the stated band and below the friction peak, so calling the action 'hold' is borderline. Below the peak the wheel is under-braked, and a real ABS controller would normally keep building (or hold only briefly) to push slip up toward 0.15. As written, a student may learn that a wheel below the peak should be held.
- ch 4 [low] Section 1, first paragraph: 'The wheel's contact patch tries to move at ωr' is loose and can mislead: in free rolling the contact patch is instantaneously at rest on the road. What is meant is the tread surface speed relative to the axle.
- ch 5 [low] Section 2 (sensors): The text says the steering angle sensor at the column measures δ, but δ was defined in Section 1 as the road-wheel steering angle. A column sensor measures the steering-wheel angle, which is larger than δ by the steering ratio, so a student may think δ comes straight from the sensor.
- ch 5 [low] Section 1 (sign of the error): The error e is used with a sign ('positive e means understeer'), and the quiz answers rely on turn direction, but the chapter never states which direction of yaw rate is counted positive. Without that, the sign rule cannot be reasoned out, only memorised.
- ch 5 [low] Lead: 'Some syllabi call it ESC or VDC, for vehicle dynamics control' reads as if both abbreviations expand to 'vehicle dynamics control'. Only VDC does; ESC stands for electronic stability control.
- ch 6 [high] Worked example 1, step 4 and answer: Part (c) asks to 'state the damper limit', but no limit is given anywhere in the question. Step 4 then declares 1.0 A to be 'the damper limit' out of nowhere, and it is not even clear whether the limit is a current (1.0 A) or a force (800 N). A student has no way to reproduce this number.
- ch 6 [low] Section 1 (semi-active) and exam trap 3: 'Never push the body' and 'cannot create force in the direction of motion' are loose. A semi-active damper can and does apply an upward force on the body; what it cannot do is add energy. Its force always opposes the relative velocity between body and wheel, not the body's absolute velocity, so the phrase 'direction of motion' is ambiguous.
- ch 6 [low] Section 2 and figure node 'Body velocity': Body velocity is shown as if measured directly. An accelerometer measures acceleration; velocity comes from integrating that signal.
- ch 6 [low] Section 3, Bandwidth: Saying the loop must respond at 'several times the body's 1-2 Hz ride frequency' suggests about 5-10 Hz. That is below the wheel-hop frequency (roughly 10-15 Hz), which the same controller must also handle, so a student may size the loop too slow.
- ch 6 [low] Section 2, after the skyhook formula: The formula is written as the command, but in a semi-active system that force can only be produced when it is dissipative. The clipping rule is missing, and it is the step that connects the law to the hardware the chapter describes.
- ch 7 [high] Section 1 (crash pulse and crumple zone), the 'Without crush' sentence: The numbers do not follow from the formula. At 13.9 m/s with only a few millimetres of crush (say 5 mm), a = v² ÷ (2s) is about 19,000 m/s², which is roughly 2000 g, and t = v ÷ a is about 0.7 ms, not 'a few milliseconds'. The sentence understates both deceleration and time by an order of magnitude.
- ch 7 [high] Lead: 'Every car on Indian roads today carries this chain, from a small hatchback to a bus' is wrong. Many older cars in use have no airbag, and buses in India do not carry airbags; they rely on structure and, where fitted, belts.
- ch 7 [low] Section 3 (inflator and venting): 'The bag never stops anyone' is too strong. The bag does apply force to the head and chest; what it cannot do is arrest the whole body, which is the belt's job. A student may write 'the airbag applies no force'.
- ch 7 [low] Quiz Q2 answer: 'Both must close or cross threshold' is loose. The accelerometer does not close; it crosses a stored threshold. Only the safing sensor closes.
- ch 8 [low] Lead: Saying ADAS 'acts for a moment' is misleading. Adaptive cruise and lane keeping act continuously, not momentarily, and a student may think ADAS gives only a single short pulse of control.
- ch 8 [low] Section 1, last paragraph and lead: The sentence 'Only a few universities teach this as a full chapter' appears twice (lead and Section 1), which reads as padding.
- ch 8 [low] Section 1, first paragraph: 'The figure runs from raw data at the bottom to decision at the top' does not match the stack: the top layer is actuation (action), and the side label says 'decision'. A student will look for a decision layer that is not there.
- ch 8 [low] Section 2, paragraph 2: 'Radar sees a stopped car that a camera misses in low sun' uses a weak radar case. Detecting stationary objects is radar's hard case because of stationary clutter (guard rails, signs, bridges); its solid strength is range and closing speed in rain, fog and glare.
- ch 8 [low] Section 2, paragraph 3: 'AEB, lane keep and adaptive cruise act; the rest mostly warn or assist' is not accurate. Parking assist steers and brakes, and some blind spot systems brake; these are interventions.
- ch 8 [low] Exam traps, unit-conversion bullet: 'Mixing units changes stopping distance by a factor of 3.6 or about 13' is vague. The factor is 3.6 on the delay term v·t_d and 3.6² ≈ 13 on the braking term v²/(2a), so the total is not scaled by a single clean factor.
- ch 9 [high] Worked example 1, step 5 and answer (part c): The proportional-only offset is computed as b·v_ref/Kp = 3000/500 = 6 m/s. This is not the steady state of the plant given in step 1. With u = Kp·e the vehicle settles where 500(30 − v) = 100v, i.e. v = 25 m/s, so the true offset is 5 m/s. The step silently assumes the resistance stays at its 30 m/s value while the speed has dropped, and so overstates the offset.
- ch 9 [low] Section 1, sentence 'to hold v_ref, force b v_ref needs error b v_ref/Kp': Stated as an equality, this is the same approximation as in the worked example; the exact relation is e_ss = b·v_ref/(Kp + b).
- ch 9 [low] Quiz Q5: The answer 6 m/s comes from the same shortcut. The exact offset is 2400/(400 + b), which is below 6 m/s and cannot be found because b (or v_ref) is not given. A student who solves the loop exactly cannot match the given answer.
- ch 9 [low] Section 2, derivative filter sentence: 'The filter is Tf s + 1 in the derivative path' can be misread as multiplying the derivative by (Tf s + 1), which adds differentiation. The filter is a lag placed on the derivative, so the path is s/(Tf s + 1).
- ch 9 [low] Section 1 and Exam traps, Ki units: 'Ki is N per (m/s·s)' is ambiguous; with e in m/s and ∫e dt in m, Ki is simply N/m (equivalently N per metre of integrated speed error).
- ch 9 [low] Section 1, reference model sentence and Section 2 last paragraph: With the ideal lambda-tuned loop the closed loop is exactly first order, 1/(1 + λs), which has no overshoot. Adding a reference filter of the same λ makes the response 1/(1 + λs)², which has about 4 % overshoot, so 'overshoot falls' is not true for the loop as modelled. The real benefit is a gentler force demand that avoids actuator saturation.
