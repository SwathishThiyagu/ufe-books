## Reviewer notes (after any revision)

- ch 1 [low] Figure (spec): The capacitor is drawn as a block in the forward path after the contact breaker (edge cb → cap, dashed). A student can read this as the capacitor being in series with the points. The ballast resistor, which the text says feeds the primary, does not appear at all, and no return path to ground/battery is shown for the contact breaker or the plugs.
- ch 1 [low] Section 4 and Quiz Q7: The vacuum advance unit is said to be 'linked to the inlet manifold' and the quiz answer says light load gives high manifold vacuum. Most contact-point systems take ported vacuum from a drilling just above the throttle plate, so the signal is near zero at idle and rises at part throttle. Stated as manifold vacuum, a student may expect full advance at idle, which is not how these systems are set up.
- ch 1 [low] Section 3: The symbol θ_spark appears in 'dwell angle in crank degrees = dwell fraction × θ_spark' without being named as a symbol or given a value rule, so the reader must infer it from the words 'angle between sparks'.
- ch 1 [low] Lead: 'The same mechanism advances or retards the spark as speed and load change' has no clear antecedent; the contact breaker does not advance timing, the centrifugal and vacuum units do.
- ch 2 [high] Lead: It says the firing voltage rises with electrode temperature. Hot electrodes emit electrons more easily and lower the firing voltage; Section 2 and standard spark-plug theory say Vf falls as the electrodes get hotter.
- ch 2 [high] Worked example 1: The question says the plug 'needs 22 kV at a 0.9 mm gap on full load', but step 5 ignores 22 kV and takes Vreq = 29.8 kV from the full-load line. With a 22 kV requirement, the 25 kV coil would fire, so the answer contradicts the data given.
- ch 2 [low] Worked example 1, steps 1 and 2: '8 × 10−3 H' can be read as 8 × (10 minus 3) H, not 8 × 10⁻³ H. The same notation appears in step 2.
- ch 2 [low] Lead: 'A spark plug is a sealed gap' is unclear; the plug seals the combustion chamber and provides a gap, but the gap itself is not a seal. 'Must jump a spark' is also awkward.
- ch 2 [low] Lead: Spark is timed shortly before top dead centre, not exactly 'at the moment the piston is near top dead centre'. The wording may suggest TDC firing.
- ch 2 [low] Figure and Quiz Q4: The full-load line gives 34.2 kV at 1.1 mm, above the stated typical band 10–30 kV. Q4 then asks for this value without noting that the line is a high-compression example.
- ch 3 [high] Exam traps, trap 1: The trap contradicts the chapter and is technically wrong. Section 1 already says a constant-dwell system holds dwell time nearly fixed, yet the trap says 'Constant-energy systems hold dwell time instead'. A constant-energy system does not hold dwell time; it varies dwell time to reach a target coil energy (longer dwell at low battery voltage, shorter when time is short). A student reading both will be confused about which system fixes time and which fixes energy.
- ch 3 [low] Section 1, constant-energy paragraph: 'At high speed it may shorten dwell to avoid overheating the coil' gives the wrong main reason. At high speed the limit is that the time between sparks becomes short, so the driver has to shorten dwell; coil heating is mainly a low-speed, long-dwell concern with a high dwell fraction.
- ch 3 [low] Lead paragraph: 'Corrects them for engine speed, manifold pressure, coolant temperature and knock' is loose. Speed and manifold pressure are the axes of the look-up map itself, not corrections added on top of a mapped value. Knock, coolant temperature and starting are the corrections.
- ch 4 [high] Quiz Q5: The answer contradicts the chapter itself. Section 1 calls waste spark one form of distributorless ignition, and Section 2 calls direct ignition the other form, so waste spark is a type, not another name for the whole system. 'Electronic ignition without a distributor' is a description, not a term.
- ch 4 [low] Figure caption: The caption says each bar lasts 60° but never says what the bar stands for. A reader can take 60° crank rotation as the spark duration, which is far longer than a real spark (typically 1 to 2 ms, well under 40° at these speeds).
- ch 4 [low] Quiz Q4: 'needs one coil and cam phase per cylinder' reads as if every cylinder needs its own cam sensor. One cam sensor supplies phase for all cylinders.
- ch 5 [high] Section 3, lamp units and demands: The asymmetric beam direction is reversed. In a left-hand-drive country the driver sits on the left and traffic runs on the right, so the kerb (near side) is on the right; the beam must rise on the right and stay low and short on the left, towards oncoming traffic. The text says it is low on the right in left-hand-drive countries while also claiming this lights the near side, which contradicts itself. India drives on the left, so Indian lamps are mirrored again.
- ch 5 [high] Quiz Q4: The question fixes the reflector as the same for both lamps, but the answer explains the difference by saying the halogen beam may be narrower. With the same reflector the beam shape is essentially the same, so the real reason the 1500 lm halogen looks brighter is simply that it puts about 1.5 times the flux into the same beam. The given answer does not answer the question as posed.
- ch 5 [low] Section 2, table row for Laser: The efficacy cell 'very high' is unquantified, so a student cannot compare it with the numeric ranges given for the other sources.
- ch 6 [high] Exam traps, first bullet: The trap has the physics backwards. Current through a lamp rises when the supply voltage rises. Two 55 W lamps rated at 12 V take about 9.2 A at 12 V but about 10.6 A at 13.8–14.4 V. So calculating at 12 V gives a current that is too low, not too high. As printed, a student learns the opposite of what happens, and would size a fuse too small.
- ch 6 [low] Quiz Q3: The step P = 110 W at 13.8 V treats the headlamps as a constant-power load. The 55 W rating is at 12 V, so each lamp has R = V²/P = 144 ÷ 55 = 2.62 Ω, and at 13.8 V the real total current is about 10.5 A, not 7.97 A. The answer matches the usual exam convention but contradicts the power-versus-voltage point made in the traps.
- ch 6 [low] Section 1, colour rule and fog lamp sentence: Two statements clash. Front fog lamps are called white or amber, and then the rule says front lamps are white or amber 'except amber indicators', which leaves no amber allowed except indicators. Amber at the front is reserved for direction indicators; front fog lamps are white or selective yellow.
- ch 6 [low] Figure (Lighting circuit path): The dashed branch is drawn from the lighting switch to the flasher unit, which suggests the lighting switch feeds the indicators. Indicators have their own indicator switch, and the flasher is fed through the hazard switch. The indicator lamps node also has no earth return edge, unlike the lamp node.
- ch 7 [high] Worked example 1, steps 1–3 (and Q6 which uses the same method): The lever arm is wrong. The rear drops 20 mm while the front axle stays on the road, so the body rotates about the front axle. The pitch angle is drop divided by the wheelbase, not drop divided by the rear-axle-to-headlamp distance. The lamp's position along the car does not change the body rotation angle (every point of a rigid body turns by the same angle), and a headlamp 2.0 m ahead of the rear axle is not a real geometry anyway. Q6 repeats the same wrong lever arm.
- ch 7 [high] Worked example 1, steps 4–5, and Q5, against Exam trap 2: Step 4 computes the road wheel angle (5.625°) and then step 5 ignores it, applying the gain to the steering wheel angle (0.15 × 90°). Q5 does the same (finds 4° road wheel angle, then uses 60°). But the second exam trap tells students to 'divide by the steering ratio first', i.e. to use the road wheel angle. A student cannot tell which angle the gain acts on.
- ch 7 [low] Worked example 1, step 3: 'The lamp rises by 0.57°' mixes a height with an angle. Loading does not lift the lamp much; it tilts the beam axis up.
- ch 7 [low] Worked example 1, given data: The 75 kg boot load is never used; only the resulting 20 mm drop matters, so students may hunt for a calculation that does not exist.
- ch 8 [low] Exam traps, first trap: The trap says a change of final drive changes the reading. That is true only when the speed sensor sits at the gearbox output. When the sensor is at the wheels, changing the final drive ratio does not change the pulses per km, so the speedometer reading stays the same. A student can carry away a wrong rule.
- ch 8 [low] Section 2 and Quiz Q1: Section 2 defines C as the tyre rolling circumference and N as pulses per wheel revolution, which is correct for a wheel sensor. For a gearbox-output sensor the same formula needs the distance travelled per revolution of that shaft, that is, tyre circumference divided by the final drive ratio. The chapter does not say this, so the formula is applied blindly to both sensor positions. Separately, Q1 answers that the coolant gauge is in °C while it is marked C to H, which contradicts Section 1, where the gauge has no unit and only end marks.
- ch 8 [low] Quiz Q3 answer: "Its return detent" is unclear. Cluster stepper motors have no return spring; the needle comes back because the driver chip steps the motor back. As written, a student may think a spring or detent brings the needle home.
- ch 9 [high] Figure (sequence diagram) and its caption in Section 3: The figure mixes the remote keyless entry link with the immobiliser check. The fob's rolling-code frame is not validated by the immobiliser ECU — it is demodulated and checked by the receiver/body control module. The counter does not advance only after the engine is running: fob and receiver advance it on every valid press. The message 'Immobiliser ECU → Receiver: advance counter' is therefore wrong, and it contradicts the body text, which says the rolling code changes on every press.
- ch 9 [low] Worked example 1, step 5 and answer; Quiz Q3 answer: The value 1.5708 m/s is the average tip speed over the sweep, but the answer says the tip 'moves at' that speed. The instantaneous speed of the tip varies through the arc (zero at the ends, maximum at mid-sweep), so a student can read this as a constant speed.
- ch 9 [low] Section 3, last paragraph: The electric horn paragraph does not belong with keyless entry and the immobiliser; only the central-locking sentence is relevant to the RKE command. The horn also has no wiring link explained beyond the vague 'same wiring', which a student cannot use.
