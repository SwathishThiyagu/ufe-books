## Reviewer notes (after any revision)

- ch 1 [low] Section 2, sentence after the D formula: The reason given for the 1/(1−ν²) factor is muddled and can mislead. The stiffening applies only when the plate is held from curving sideways (cylindrical bending, Section 3). In the free-edge case used in the worked example the plate is not stiffer than a beam strip: Mx = Dκx(1−ν²) = Et³κx/12, the beam value. The sentence 'bending in x also stretches the plate in y and Poisson's effect resists that stretch' describes the opposite of the restraint that causes the stiffening.
- ch 1 [low] Section 2, twisting moment relation: The chapter defines a moment per unit width as stress times z summed through the thickness, but with κxy = −2∂²w/∂x∂y that integration gives Mxy = D(1−ν)κxy/2, not D(1−ν)κxy. The printed pair is the total-twisting-moment convention (Mxy + Myx), so the derivation sentence and the formula disagree by a factor of 2.
- ch 1 [low] Figure, node d note: The note 'D = Et³/12(1−ν²)' is ambiguous: read left to right it means (Et³/12)(1−ν²), which is wrong.
- ch 1 [low] Figure, edge from m to d: The arrow runs from Moments M to Plate stiffness D and is labelled 'uses', which reverses cause and effect: D sets the moments, the moments do not use D.
- ch 1 [low] Beyond paragraph: 'Sandwich faces add a core shear term to D' is wrong. The faces set the bending stiffness D, while the core adds transverse shear flexibility, which is a separate shear stiffness (shear correction), not a term inside D.
- ch 2 [high] Section 2, edge-condition table, row 'Free': The free-edge row gives 'effective shear = 0, twisting moment = 0' and drops the bending moment. In thin-plate (Kirchhoff) theory the two free-edge conditions are: normal bending moment Mx = 0, and effective shear Vx = Qx + ∂Mxy/∂y = 0. The twisting moment Mxy is NOT zero on a free edge; it is absorbed into the effective shear. Listing both 'effective shear' and 'twisting moment = 0' mixes the Kirchhoff reduction with the unreduced Poisson conditions, so a student would impose one condition too many and the wrong one.
- ch 2 [low] Section 1, sentence after the stress-resultant definition: 'Nxy twists the plate and couples the x and y curvatures' is imprecise. The Nxy term multiplies the twisting curvature ∂²w/∂x∂y, which is a single mixed curvature, not a coupling of the two direct curvatures.
- ch 2 [low] Section 1, last sentence: 'Some syllabi call this plates under bending, twisting and transverse loads' is vague and does not say what 'this' refers to; it reads like a stray remark after the Nxy sentence.
- ch 2 [low] Figure spec, node 'eq': The note on the plate-equation block reads '∇⁴w = q/D', which is the pure-bending equation with no membrane term. The figure is about combined loading, so the label contradicts Section 1.
- ch 2 [low] Section 3, paragraph on the 0.00406 coefficient, and Exam trap 4: The text implies the 0.00406 deflection coefficient is valid only for ν = 0.30. For simply supported edges the deflection of a uniformly loaded plate is independent of ν: w = 0 and M = 0 on a straight edge force ∇²w = 0 there, and ν never appears in that boundary condition. Tables quote ν = 0.30 because they list moments (which do depend on ν) next to the deflection.
- ch 3 [low] Section 1, formula definitions: The definition says subscripts xx, xy, yy mean second partial derivatives, but the same line uses wxxxx, wxxyy and wyyyy, which are fourth derivatives. A student can read wxxyy as two second derivatives or as a second derivative, and the notation is not explained.
- ch 3 [low] Section 2, boundary conditions: The statement 'cannot take moment, so the second derivative normal to each edge is zero' is only true for an isotropic plate of constant thickness; the chapter never states the isotropic, homogeneous, constant-thickness assumption behind D = Et³/[12(1−ν²)] and k = 4.
- ch 3 [low] Section 3, aspect-ratio discussion: 'At a/b=1.5, no integer matches, so k is above 4' gives no number, so the reader cannot check the claim or see how far above 4 it is.
- ch 3 [low] Section 3, 'm is the integer nearest a/b': The 'nearest integer' rule is ambiguous at half-integer aspect ratios such as a/b = 1.5 or 2.5, where two integers are equally near.
- ch 3 [low] Exam trap 4: 'k=4 only when a/b is an integer m' is stated without the condition that it applies to a plate with all four edges simply supported and n = 1. Without that condition a student may carry k = 4 over to clamped or partly restrained panels.
- ch 4 [high] Section 1, last lines: The sentence 'This is why a real compression panel can be lighter than a solid sheet of the same total area' is wrong as written. A stiffened panel of the same plan area carries more material (skin plus stringers) and so is heavier; a sheet of the same material area weighs the same. The correct comparison is at equal load, not equal area.
- ch 4 [low] Worked example 1: The step list tells the student to limit beff to p/2, but the example never checks this limit because no pitch is given. A student may think the check is optional.
- ch 4 [low] Section 2, p/2 limit sentence: 'if it does, the whole sheet is effective and the skin has not buckled over the full bay' is vague: the effective zones from neighbouring stringers meet, and the applied stress is at or below the local buckling stress, so the whole bay p is counted.
- ch 5 [high] Section 1, plate buckling coefficient in the σel formula: The only value of k given is k = 4.0, for a long plate simply supported on both long edges. The same section says a flange has one long edge supported and one long edge free, so a student will put k = 4.0 into σel for a flange and get a stress about 9 times too high. For a long flange with one edge free, k is about 0.43, not 4.0.
- ch 5 [low] Section 1, last sentence: "It lies between σel and σcy for most practical sections" is only true when σel is below σcy. For a stocky element, σel (from the plate formula) is above σcy, so σcrip is capped near σcy and is lower than σel.
- ch 5 [low] Section 3 heading and step list: The heading promises the Gerard method, but the section is a general design procedure that mixes sizing, chart reading, inter-rivet buckling and wrinkling. The numbered steps start with no lead-in sentence, so the reader cannot see what the list is.
- ch 5 [low] Figure: The caption calls the figure a crippling chart, but the plot shows only one point at b/t = 15 and the yield cut-off line. There is no σcrip/σcy against b/t curve, so the fall of the curve as b/t rises is not shown.
- ch 6 [high] Section 1, paragraph 2: It says the axial load through C 'produces a torque P y0 (and P x0), which rotates the section'. A force parallel to the column axis cannot produce a moment about that axis while the column is straight: P y0 is a moment about a transverse axis, that is a bending moment, not a twisting moment. A student will also be confused by the worked example, where x0 = 0 and y0 = 20 mm, so P y0 = 20P would be a large standing torque that the example ignores.
- ch 6 [high] Section 2, paragraph 3: 'For a singly symmetric section, flexure about the symmetry axis uncouples, but the other two modes still interact' is the reverse of the standard result. Bending about the axis of symmetry is the mode that couples with torsion; bending in the plane of symmetry (about the axis normal to the axis of symmetry) is the mode that uncouples. For a channel with a vertical web, the symmetry axis is horizontal (major axis), and that is exactly the bending that mixes with twist.
- ch 6 [low] Quiz Q3: The answer repeats the same torque error: 'the load has a torque arm about S, P y0 or P x0'. Taken literally, this says a straight column loaded through C is under a twisting moment.
- ch 6 [low] Section 2, paragraph 2: Px and Py appear in 'a 3×3 determinant in Px, Py and Pt' but are never defined; only Pt is defined in the boxed formula.
- ch 6 [low] Figure: The figure uses a single 'Offset e' while the text uses x0 and y0, so the two do not connect.
- ch 7 [high] Section 2, last paragraph ('Do not call Q11 a modulus'): The sentence says Q11, Q12 and Q22 'come from a test where the ply is free to contract sideways'. This is the reverse of the definition. The free-contraction (uniaxial, σ2 = 0) test gives E1, E2 and ν12. The reduced stiffnesses Q11, Q12, Q22 correspond to the condition ε2 = 0, i.e. the ply is held against sideways contraction. That constraint is exactly why Q11 is larger than E1, and the sentence contradicts both the formula and the earlier paragraph.
- ch 7 [low] Section 2, paragraph beginning 'The denominator appears because...': Calling Q11 'the real stiffness' clashes with the next paragraph's warning that Q11 must not be called a modulus, and the chain 'contraction reduces the stress needed, so Q11 is larger than E1' is hard to follow for a first-time reader.
- ch 7 [low] Section 3, paragraph on Qbar16 and Qbar26: 'largest near 45°' is inaccurate. For a carbon/epoxy ply (Q11 = 140.91, Q22 = 10.06, Q12 = 3.02, Q66 = 5 GPa) Qbar16 peaks near 30° and Qbar26 near 60°, not at 45°.
- ch 7 [low] Exam traps, first bullet: The reason given for a bigger error in glass plies is wrong: glass/epoxy ν12 (about 0.26) is not higher than carbon/epoxy ν12 (about 0.30). The error grows because E2/E1 is larger for glass, and even then it is only about 1.4%, not 'much larger'.
- ch 8 [high] Section 3, Worked example 1 step 6, Exam traps: EI = bD11 is the cylindrical-bending plate stiffness, not the bending stiffness of a narrow beam with free lateral surfaces. For a narrow symmetric beam, M2 = 0 gives κ2 = −(D12/D22)κ1 and effective stiffness EI = b(D11 − D12²/D22). The text calls bD11 a narrow-beam result and says a wide beam needs more, which is at best incomplete and can overestimate beam stiffness.
- ch 8 [low] Worked example 1 step 4: The bracketed expression 140.9[(0.25)³−(−0.125)³] is not the per-interval formula and would give a wrong value if followed. The question mark makes the step unclear even though the correct values are used afterwards.
- ch 8 [low] Section 1: The sentence 'D weights it by z³' is imprecise: a ply at the mid-plane still contributes to D through its own thickness. D uses the difference zk³−zk−1³, which for a thin ply is about 3z²t.
- ch 9 [high] Section 1, paragraph on damping ratio (the sentence 'Aircraft structures are lightly damped: ζ is often a few percent. ζ  1 is overdamped.'): The comparison sign is missing, so the sentence reads 'ζ  1 is overdamped' and the student cannot tell which case is meant. The underdamped and critically damped cases are also never stated.
- ch 9 [low] Section 3, divergence formula: Cmα is not signed. For a stable wing section Cmα is negative about the elastic axis, so a student who substitutes the usual value gets a negative qD and a square root of a negative number. The chapter should say the magnitudes are used.
- ch 10 [low] Lead (first paragraph): The sentence 'The shear forces the tilt the plate into a diagonal wave' is grammatically broken and reads as if 'forces' is a noun; a student may stumble on it.
- ch 10 [low] Lead and Section 2, first paragraph: 'the two loads help each other' is ambiguous. It can be misread as shear increasing the compression the plate can carry, when the point is that each load reduces the capacity left for the other.
- ch 10 [low] Section 2, interaction equation paragraph: The fully quadratic form is presented as 'the usual energy-based estimate', but several aircraft structures references use an interaction that is linear in compression and quadratic in shear, Rc + Rs² = 1 (Rc = Nx/Nx,cr, Rs = Nxy/Nxy,cr). That curve is more restrictive; at Rs = 0.5 it gives Rc = 0.75, whereas the fully quadratic gives 0.866, so the quadratic is unconservative against it.
- ch 10 [low] Worked example 1, question and answer: 54.3 kN/m is the shear at buckling, not an allowable value; 'allowable' normally means a value already divided by a factor of safety, so a student may think a safety factor has been applied.
- ch 10 [low] Quiz Q3, answer: 'Square = 0.36' is unclear shorthand; it is not stated what is being squared.
