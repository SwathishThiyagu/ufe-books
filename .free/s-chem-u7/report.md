## Reviewer notes (after any revision)

- ch 1 [low] Section 2 and Worked example 1: The equivalent weights are given as bare numbers (Ca²⁺ = 20, Mg²⁺ = 12, CaCO₃ = 50) with no unit. A student can read them as molar masses, which is exactly the error the 'Exam traps' section warns about, and the formula H = m × 50 ÷ E then looks dimensionally wrong.
- ch 1 [low] Section 3, last sentence: 'Only a few universities teach this chapter in full depth' is a meta remark dropped into the middle of a procedure, and 'this chapter' is ambiguous (the water chapter, or the titration?). It also reads oddly, since the paragraph is about indicator chemistry, not about a chapter.
- ch 1 [low] Section 1, table: The text lists chloride, sulphate, nitrate and iron as measured chemical parameters, but the table gives no limits for them. A student may conclude these have no BIS limit, or that only the six listed parameters are regulated.
- ch 1 [low] Section 2, classification: The ranges overlap at 120 mg/L: 'moderately hard 60–120' and 'hard 120–180' both contain 120, so a value of exactly 120 falls in two classes.
- ch 2 [high] Section 2, lime formula: The lime formula omits the magnesium non-carbonate term even though the reaction list includes MgSO₄ + Ca(OH)₂ → Mg(OH)₂ + CaSO₄. For a water containing MgSO₄ or other magnesium non-carbonate hardness, the formula underestimates lime.
- ch 2 [high] Lead, Section 1, Quiz Q1: pH 10–11 is normally a boiler-water target, not a feed-water target. Feed water is usually kept near pH 8.5–9.5; boiler water is kept alkaline near pH 10–11. As written, students will memorize the wrong feed-water limit.
- ch 2 [low] Quiz Q5: Hydrazine and sodium sulphite are chemical oxygen scavengers, not deaerators. A deaerator is mechanical equipment.
- ch 3 [low] Section 1 (screening) and the figure note for node b: Fine screens are said to stop grit. Grit is sand and silt; it passes through screens and is removed later in a grit chamber by settling, so a student may wrongly think screens remove grit.
- ch 3 [low] Section 1 (Stokes' law): Stokes' law is stated without its validity limit. It holds only for slow, laminar settling of discrete (non-flocculating) spherical particles, roughly Reynolds number below 1; outside this the calculated v is wrong.
- ch 3 [low] Quiz Q4: The answer only restates the pH band ("floc forms best near neutral pH") and then talks about alkalinity, so it does not actually explain why 6.5–7.5 is the best band. A student copying this gets little credit for an "analyze" question.
- ch 4 [low] Section 2, paragraph 1: "In one compartment the cation membrane lets cations out and the anion membrane blocks them" — "them" is ambiguous; a student may read it as the anion membrane blocking anions, which is the opposite of what an anion-exchange membrane does.
- ch 4 [low] Section 1, paragraph 4: "A module never converts all the feed" uses "converts" loosely; the chapter has just defined the correct term, recovery, and mixing the two words confuses the definition.
- ch 4 [low] Comparison table, row 'Fouling': The electrodialysis cell gives a control method ('polarity reversal') where the reverse-osmosis cell gives fouling types ('scale and biofouling'); the two halves of the row do not compare the same thing.
- ch 4 [low] Beyond box: "Electrodialysis reverses into electrodialysis reversal for difficult feeds" reads as if the stack turns into a different process; polarity reversal is an operating mode of the same stack.
