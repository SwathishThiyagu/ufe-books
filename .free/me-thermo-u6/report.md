## Reviewer notes (after any revision)

- ch 2 [low] Exam traps, third bullet: The sentence "Putting 300°C instead of 573 K gives a wrong r^(γ−1)" is self-contradictory: 300 °C is the same temperature as 573 K, so a student can read it as saying the kelvin value 573 K is the mistake. The real mistake is dropping the °C and using the bare number 300 in the ratio T₂/T₁.
- ch 2 [low] Figure: The plotted points put state 1 at v = 8 m³/kg and state 2 at v = 1 m³/kg, but the caption ties the pressures to the worked example, where p₁ = 1 bar and T₁ = 300 K give v₁ = RT₁/p₁ = 0.287 × 300/100 = 0.861 m³/kg and v₂ = 0.108 m³/kg. A student who checks the numbers will find the volume axis disagrees with the example by a factor of about 9.
- ch 2 [low] Exam traps, first bullet: The trap defines r_c = v₃/v₂ while telling students not to confuse it with r in Diesel or Dual. In the Dual cycle the cutoff ratio is v₄/v₃, not v₃/v₂, so the single definition does not cover the Dual cycle it is meant to protect.
- ch 3 [low] Worked example 1, step 1, and Quiz Q3: The exponent markup is missing. Step 1 prints 'x = rp(γ−1)/γ = 80.4/1.4 = 80.2857 = 1.811', and Q3 prints '300 × 40.2857'. On a phone these read as the plain numbers 80.2857 and 40.2857, which are not equal to 1.811 or 1.486, so the arithmetic looks wrong. Section 1 uses superscript tags correctly, so this is an inconsistency inside the chapter.
- ch 3 [low] Lead paragraph: 'Both cycles appear in aircraft engines, power plants, and solar or waste-heat engines' lumps the two cycles together. A student can read this as saying Stirling engines are used in aircraft engines, which is not true.
- ch 4 [high] Section 3, paragraph 'Same compression ratio': There is a leftover drafting correction inside the text: 'Efficiency of any cycle is 1 − T_in,mean /... no: it is 1 − T_out,mean / T_in,mean'. A student reading this on a phone sees two contradictory formulas and a broken sentence.
- ch 4 [high] Section 3, paragraph 'Same compression ratio': The explanation says constant pressure addition 'puts the last part of the heat in while the gas is expanding and cooling'. During constant pressure heat addition the gas does expand, but its temperature keeps rising (dq = cp dT with dT > 0). There is no cooling, so the stated reason for the lower T_in,mean is wrong.
- ch 4 [low] Section 2, paragraph 'MEP rises with r at fixed heat input': The wording 'the efficiency gain is larger than the loss of swept volume' is confusing: the swept volume does not fall, it grows with r, and that growth pushes MEP down rather than being a loss that has already happened. The quiz Q7 answer states the same idea correctly, so the two places disagree in wording.
- ch 5 [high] Exam traps, first trap: The factor is wrong. Putting the bar value 39.9 into the kPa formula gives 0.001010 × 39.9 = 0.0403 kJ/kg against the correct 4.03 kJ/kg, so the result is one hundred times too small, not a thousand times. 1 bar = 100 kPa.
- ch 5 [low] Section 2 and Exam traps item 3: The dryness formula x₄ = (s₃ − s_f)/s_fg is used without checking that the exhaust is actually wet. If s₃ ≥ s_g at condenser pressure the exhaust is superheated and the formula fails (x₄ would come out above 1).
- ch 5 [low] Quiz Q5 answer: 'less heat is rejected for the same work' is muddled. Lowering the condenser pressure keeps the heat input nearly fixed, produces more turbine work, and so rejects less heat.
- ch 6 [low] Section 3, open feedwater heater energy balance: The symbols h_2 and h_3 are reused with new meanings. In Section 2 and the worked example, h_2 is the pump exit and h_3 is the boiler exit (3213.6 kJ/kg). In Section 3, h_2 becomes the feedwater entering the heater and h_3 the saturated liquid leaving it. A student flipping between the sections will read the heater balance with the wrong numbers.
- ch 6 [low] Figure 'Reheat and one bleed': The feed heater sits between the pump and the boiler with no pump after it, and the boiler note still says '2→3, heat in' and the heater note says 'bleed warms 2'. That contradicts the text's own rule that each open heater needs a pump after it, and it contradicts the state numbering, because the fluid entering the boiler is no longer state 2.
- ch 6 [low] Section 3, efficiency argument: The Carnot limit is written as 1 - Treject/Tmean,in without saying that both temperatures must be absolute (kelvin). Students often substitute 45 °C and 220 °C directly and get a nonsense answer.
- ch 6 [low] Section 1, reheat temperature range: 'back to about the original 400-540 °C' understates modern practice; reheat temperature is normally chosen equal to, or close to, the initial steam temperature, which in current plants is about 540 °C and up to about 600 °C in ultra-supercritical units.
- ch 7 [high] Section 1, last paragraph: "A pump would cavitate on a mixture; a compressor can handle it" says a compressor can tolerate a two-phase mixture. That is wrong: liquid droplets entering a compressor cause slugging and valve damage, which is exactly why superheat is used (as the chapter itself says in Section 2). The sentence also contradicts the line just before it, that the compressor handles vapour, not a mixture.
- ch 7 [low] Section 3, T-s diagram paragraph: "Condensation 2→3 lies inside the dome" is not true as stated. Compression 1→2 starts at saturated vapour and, being isentropic, ends superheated, so state 2 sits outside the dome; only the later part of 2→3 (the condensation itself) is the horizontal line inside the dome.
- ch 7 [low] Lead: "read from enthalpy values, not from steam tables alone" suggests steam tables are partly used for a refrigerant. Refrigerant properties come from refrigerant tables or charts, not steam tables.
- ch 7 [low] Exam traps, second bullet: "Using h3 − h4 as work" is a confusing trap as written, because h3 = h4, so the quantity is simply zero. The mistake actually worth warning about is taking the condenser heat h2 − h3 as the work input.
- ch 8 [low] Section 2 heading and opening line: The heading and the first sentence say 'six components', but the text then names seven: generator, condenser, expansion valve, evaporator, absorber, pump and solution heat exchanger. The figure shows only six (no solution heat exchanger), so a student cannot tell whether the count is wrong or the heat exchanger is not being counted.
- ch 8 [low] Exam traps, second trap: The trapped expression TE/(TG − TE) is the Carnot COP of a work-driven refrigerator running between TE and TG. It is not the reversible limit of an absorption cycle, which involves three temperatures, TE, TG and the condenser/absorber temperature TC. A student may read the trap as saying that TE/(TG − TE) is the correct reversible absorption COP, only misapplied.
