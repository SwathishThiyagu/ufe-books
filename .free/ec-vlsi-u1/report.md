## Reviewer notes (after any revision)

- ch 1 [high] Section 1, paragraph on accumulation: The direction of the gate voltage is wrong. For a p-type body, accumulation happens when VG is BELOW the flat-band voltage; there the negative gate charge attracts holes to the surface. A positive gate (VG above VFB) repels holes and gives depletion, not accumulation. As written, the text says 'Raise VG above VFB... the positive gate charge attracts holes, and they pile up at the surface', which contradicts both the physics and the lead of the chapter.
- ch 1 [high] Quiz Q1: The question asks for the three states 'as the gate voltage rises above the flat-band voltage'. Rising above VFB gives depletion and then inversion only; accumulation lies below VFB. The answer repeats the same mistake ('Accumulation: VG just above VFB, holes pile at the surface'), so the quiz is wrong as set.
- ch 1 [high] Section 3, region table: The comparison signs have been lost in three cells, so the table reads 'VGS T' and 'VDS GS − VT', which is meaningless. The nMOS cutoff row reads 'VGS T' instead of 'VGS < VT', the nMOS linear row reads 'VDS GS − VT' instead of 'VDS < VGS − VT', and the pMOS cutoff and linear rows lost their '<' as well.
- ch 1 [high] Quiz Q2 answer: The '<' signs are missing, so the answer reads 'Cutoff: VGS T. Linear: VGS > VT and VDS GS − VT', which a student cannot use.
- ch 1 [high] Quiz Q5 answer: Part of the answer text has been lost: it reads 'VDS = 0.5 V D = 500 µA/V² × ...', with the region test and the start of the current formula missing, so the reasoning cannot be followed. The final number 187.5 µA is correct.

- ch 2 [low] Section 3: The quoted value at VSB = 1 V for γ = 0.21 √V is slightly off. 0.45 + 0.21(√1.82 − √0.82) = 0.45 + 0.21(1.3491 − 0.9055) = 0.5431 V, so the correct rounding is 0.543 V, not 0.544 V. The answer then mixes both stories: 'smaller VT' and 'negative value under the square root'. A careful reader cannot tell which mistake is being described.
- ch 2 [low] Section 3: The text points the reader to 'the figure after Section 1', but the figure is printed at the end of the chapter, after the worked example and quiz. A student scrolling on a phone will look in the wrong place.
- ch 2 [low] Section 2: 'A pMOS needs reversed signs' is too vague to act on. A student can read it as γ becoming negative, or as VSB changing sign, and both readings break the calculation.

- ch 3 [high] Quiz Q3, answer: The region check is broken: 'Since VDS = 0.3 V D = 50 µA/V² × ...' drops the comparison and the conclusion, so the answer jumps straight into the plug-in. The arithmetic (127.5 µA) is right, but the reasoning step is missing.
- ch 4 [high] quiz Q7: The answer says mobility degradation would "make gm fall as overdrive grows". That is wrong. With µeff = µ0/(1 + θVov) and ID = ½kn′(W/L)Vov²/(1 + θVov), the transconductance is gm = kn′(W/L)·Vov(2 + θVov)/(1 + θVov)², which rises monotonically with overdrive and only levels off toward kn′/θ. It never decreases, so a student who checks the algebra will be confused, and the stated contrast with velocity saturation is false.
- ch 4 [low] section 1, definition of θ: "a process constant of a few tenths of a volt inverse for thin oxides" is awkward and the symbol is not given in unit form, unlike every other quantity in the chapter.
- ch 4 [low] exam traps, last bullet (unit slips): "a mixed set gives an answer off by 10⁴" is vague and not right for every quantity: cm²/V·s to m²/V·s is a factor 10⁻⁴, but fF/µm² to F/m² is only 10⁻³, so a student cannot tell which factor applies where.
- ch 5 [low] Section 2, first paragraph: "The figure above lists the split for the worked device" points the wrong way: the capacitance figure is placed after Section 3, i.e. below this sentence, so a student looking upward finds nothing.
- ch 5 [low] Section 1, overlap paragraph: "CGSO and CGDO grow when the lateral diffusion is large relative to L" mixes two ideas. The overlap capacitance per unit width grows with the lateral diffusion length itself (CGSO ≈ Cox·Lov); what depends on L is how large a share of the gate capacitance that overlap becomes.
- ch 5 [low] Section 1, last paragraph, and Exam traps bullet 2: "In a short-channel device the overlap part can exceed the ideal area part" (and "CGSO·W and CGDO·W can dominate the gate capacitance") is overstated for ordinary oxide devices. Since Cov = Cox·W·Lov and the area part is Cox·W·L, exceeding CoxWL needs Lov > L, which is not a realistic geometry; only in advanced nodes with very thin (or high-k) dielectrics does fringing push the overlap term near or past the channel term.
- ch 5 [low] Section 2, cutoff description: "The area capacitance goes to bulk through the depleted silicon: Cgb ≈ CoxWL" contradicts itself: if the field passes through depleted silicon, the depletion-layer capacitance is in series with the oxide and Cgb must be less than CoxWL. The ≈ CoxWL value is the first-order model that ignores that series term.
- ch 5 [low] Section 3, junction capacitance paragraph: The formula uses Cj0 for the zero-bias capacitance per unit area, but the next line switches to "The area part is CJ × area" without saying that CJ is the same quantity, so the two symbols look like different parameters.
- ch 5 [low] Quiz Q5: The answer calls Cj0, VB and m SPICE parameters. Only CJ, CJSW, PB and MJ are SPICE parameter names; Cj0, VB and m are the chapter's symbols for the same quantities.
- ch 5 [low] Quiz Q7: The question supplies "1 µm wide" and "10 Ω/square", but the model answer never uses either number, and "1 µm wide" does not say which dimension of the gate is meant.
- ch 5 [low] Section 2, third paragraph: "The sum changes with region" does not say which sum. Earlier the reader may assume Cgs + Cgd + Cgb is fixed, so the sentence needs the terms named and the cause given.
- ch 6 [low] Section 2, first paragraph: The sentence 'Field now rises by S, carriers hit velocity saturation, and the current grows only as S' attaches the wrong cause to the S factor. Current growing as S is the plain square-law result for fixed voltage (Cox rises as S, W/L unchanged). True velocity saturation makes the current grow slower than S, not exactly S, so a student learns a false link between velocity saturation and ID ∝ S.
- ch 6 [low] Section 1, the Cox and kappa lines: The symbols εox (permittivity of the oxide) and µn (electron mobility) are used without being defined, while P, C, VDD and f are defined in the formula block.
- ch 6 [low] Lead: 'Every node you name — 28 nm, 7 nm — is described by these numbers' can be read as saying the node number equals the channel length. Modern node names are labels and no longer match L.
- ch 7 [high] Section 1, last paragraph: The text says the exponential subthreshold line 'joins the square-law curve of Chapter 1.3' at VGS = Vth. The square-law expression gives ID = 0 at VGS = Vth, so the two curves cannot meet there. The exponential law holds only below Vth (roughly VGS a few tenths of a volt under Vth), the square law holds well above Vth, and the in-between region (moderate inversion) is described accurately by neither. As written, a student will believe both formulas give the same current at Vth.
- ch 7 [low] Section 2, second paragraph: 'Shorter L and narrower W shift Vth through short-channel effects' lumps two different effects together. The narrow-width effect (Vth rising as W shrinks) is not a short-channel effect.
- ch 7 [low] Section 3, GIDL paragraph: 'The current flows into the drain' is ambiguous about direction. In an nMOS with low VGS and high VDS, band-to-band tunnelling in the gate-drain overlap sends tunnelled electrons to the drain and holes to the body; the conventional current therefore flows from the drain to the body (out of the drain terminal). A student may take the sentence as a direction statement and get the sign wrong.
- ch 7 [low] Section 2, first paragraph: 'The slope of that line is the subthreshold swing S' is imprecise. On the log-current plot the slope is decades per volt; S is its reciprocal, in mV per decade. The next sentence defines it correctly, but the first statement invites the wrong unit.
- ch 8 [high] Section 1: The clause 'shrinks the depletion width so the drain reaches deeper' is backwards. Heavier body doping shrinks the depletion widths, including the drain junction depletion width, so the drain field penetrates less, not deeper. This is exactly why halo/pocket doping is used to fight short-channel effects. As written, a student learns the opposite of the standard mechanism.
- ch 8 [low] Section 3 and table: 'Fin pitch' and 'fin pitch' based cell layout are used without definition, and 'work function' is used in Section 2 without a one-line reminder of what it means. A reader meeting these first here has to guess.
- ch 8 [low] Quiz Q3: The answer gives only a partial mechanism ('drain field acts on few carriers'), which can be read as if the drain still controls the channel just with fewer carriers. The main reason a thin fully depleted body helps is that the drain field lines end on the nearby gate instead of spreading through the body, so the barrier near the source barely moves.
