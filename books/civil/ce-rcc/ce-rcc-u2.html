2.1 Singly reinforced rectangular beams

A singly reinforced rectangular beam has steel only near its tension face. At collapse the concrete above the neutral axis crushes in compression, the steel below yields in tension, and the two forces form a couple that resists the factored moment Mu. IS 456 turns this into two equations: C = T gives the neutral axis depth xu, and the couple gives the moment. Lintels and most floor beams are designed this way.

You need the stress block parameters, characteristic strengths and partial safety factors from the topic on design philosophies and material behaviour.

Assumptions and the two forces

IS 456 (clause 38.1) analyses a section at the limit state of collapse in flexure with these assumptions:

  • Plane sections remain plane, so strain varies linearly with depth.
  • The maximum strain in concrete at the outer compression fibre is εcu = 0.0035.
  • Concrete in tension is ignored.
  • Concrete stress follows the design parabola-rectangle curve, peaking at 0.67fck/1.5 = 0.446 fck.
  • Steel stress comes from its design stress-strain curve, with design yield stress fy/1.15 = 0.87 fy.
  • At collapse the tension steel strain is at least 0.87 fy/Es + 0.002.

Integrating the parabola-rectangle stress over the compression depth xu gives a force of 0.36 fck xu per unit width, acting 0.42 xu below the top fibre. Here b is the width, d the effective depth to the bar centroid and D the overall depth, in N and mm.

C = 0.36 fck b xu     T = 0.87 fy Ast     z = d − 0.42 xu

C is the concrete compression in N; T the steel tension in N; Ast the area of tension steel in mm²; z the lever arm in mm between C and T.

xu = 0.87 fy Ast / (0.36 fck b)     Mu = 0.87 fy Ast (d − 0.42 xu) = 0.36 fck b xu (d − 0.42 xu)

C = T solved for xu, then the moment of the couple in N·mm.

The ceiling: xu,max and Mu,lim

The steel only yields if the neutral axis is shallow enough. IS 456 caps it at xu,max: xu,max/d = 0.53 for Fe250, 0.48 for Fe415 and 0.46 for Fe500 (why these values, see balanced, under- and over-reinforced sections). Putting xu = xu,max into the moment equation gives the largest moment a singly reinforced section may carry:

Mu,lim = 0.36 fck b xu,max (d − 0.42 xu,max) = 0.148, 0.138 or 0.133 × fck b d²

The three factors are for Fe250, Fe415 and Fe500. For Fe415 and M25, Mu,lim = 0.138 × 25 = 3.45 N/mm² × b d².

If Mu ≤ Mu,lim, tension steel alone will do; if not, enlarge the section or add compression steel (doubly reinforced beams). Annex G of IS 456 also gives the steel directly, without first finding xu:

Mu = 0.87 fy Ast d (1 − Ast fy / (b d fck))   so   Ast = (0.5 fck / fy) [1 − √(1 − 4.6 Mu/(fck b d²))] b d

This is the same couple with the factor 0.42 × 0.87/0.36 = 1.015 rounded to 1, so it gives a steel area about 0.3 % smaller than the stress-block route.

Design steps and code limits

  1. Find the factored load wu = 1.5 × (dead + live) and the factored moment, Mu = wu L²/8 for a simply supported span L.
  2. Fix b and D (often L/12 to L/15), then d = D − cover − stirrup − half the bar.
  3. Compute Mu,lim. If Mu ≤ Mu,lim the section is singly reinforced.
  4. Find xu from Mu = 0.36 fck b xu (d − 0.42 xu) (take the smaller root), then Ast = Mu / [0.87 fy (d − 0.42 xu)]; or use the Annex G formula.
  5. Check the minimum steel Ast ≥ 0.85 b d/fy (clause 26.5.1.1) and the maximum Ast ≤ 0.04 b D.
  6. Choose bars with clear spacing at least the bar diameter and the aggregate size plus 5 mm; confirm the real d.

Analysis runs the same equations backwards: xu from C = T, then Mu from the couple, capped at Mu,lim if xu exceeds xu,max.

Clear cover is 20 mm for mild and 30 mm for moderate exposure (Table 16). The beam must still pass the serviceability checks.

Worked example

Question. A simply supported beam of effective span 5 m carries a dead load of 14 kN/m (self-weight included) and a live load of 10 kN/m. The section is 230 mm wide and 450 mm deep, with 30 mm clear cover and 8 mm stirrups. Use M25 concrete and Fe415 steel. Design the tension steel.

  1. Factored load: wu = 1.5 × (14 + 10) = 36 kN/m. Factored moment: Mu = 36 × 5²/8 = 112.5 kN·m = 112.5 × 10⁶ N·mm.
  2. Assume 20 mm bars: d = 450 − 30 − 8 − 10 = 402 mm; take d = 400 mm.
  3. xu,max = 0.48 × 400 = 192 mm. Mu,lim = 0.36 × 25 × 230 × 192 × (400 − 0.42 × 192) = 0.36 × 25 × 230 × 192 × 319.36 = 126.93 × 10⁶ N·mm = 126.93 kN·m.
  4. Mu = 112.5 kN·m is less than 126.93 kN·m, so the section is singly reinforced.
  5. Neutral axis: 112.5 × 10⁶ = 0.36 × 25 × 230 × xu × (400 − 0.42 xu), that is 869.4 xu² − 828 000 xu + 112.5 × 10⁶ = 0. The smaller root is xu = 164.2 mm (the larger root, 788 mm, lies below the beam).
  6. Lever arm z = 400 − 0.42 × 164.2 = 331.0 mm. Ast = 112.5 × 10⁶ / (0.87 × 415 × 331.0) = 941.2 mm². (The Annex G formula gives 938.2 mm².)
  7. Limits: minimum 0.85 × 230 × 400/415 = 188.4 mm², maximum 0.04 × 230 × 450 = 4140 mm²; 941.2 mm² is within them.
  8. Provide 3 bars of 20 mm: 3 × 314.2 = 942.5 mm². Clear spacing = (230 − 2 × 30 − 2 × 8 − 3 × 20)/2 = 47 mm, above the 25 mm needed for 20 mm aggregate.
  9. Check: with 942.5 mm², xu = 164.4 mm and Mu = 0.87 × 415 × 942.5 × (400 − 0.42 × 164.4) = 112.6 kN·m ≥ 112.5 kN·m.

Answer. Mu = 112.5 kN·m below Mu,lim = 126.93 kN·m; xu = 164.2 mm, Ast = 941 mm²; provide 3 bars of 20 mm (942.5 mm²) at d = 400 mm.

Worked example 2

Question. Find the moment of resistance of a beam 250 mm wide with an effective depth of 450 mm and 4 bars of 16 mm, in M20 concrete with Fe415 steel. What factored and service uniform loads can it carry on a simply supported span of 6 m?

  1. Ast = 4 × π/4 × 16² = 804.2 mm².
  2. xu = 0.87 × 415 × 804.2 / (0.36 × 20 × 250) = 290 360/1800 = 161.3 mm.
  3. xu,max = 0.48 × 450 = 216 mm. xu < xu,max, so the steel yields and the section is under-reinforced.
  4. Mu = 0.87 × 415 × 804.2 × (450 − 0.42 × 161.3) = 290 360 × 382.3 = 111.0 × 10⁶ N·mm = 111.0 kN·m.
  5. Factored load: wu = 8 Mu/L² = 8 × 111.0/6² = 24.7 kN/m; service load = 24.7/1.5 = 16.4 kN/m, self-weight included.

Answer. Mu = 111.0 kN·m; the beam carries 24.7 kN/m factored, or 16.4 kN/m of service load including its own weight.

Exam traps

  • Using D in place of d. The lever arm is measured to the steel centroid; with D = 450 mm instead of d = 400 mm the first example would give Ast = 795 mm², about 15 % too small.
  • Writing T = fy Ast. The design stress is 0.87 fy (fy divided by the material factor 1.15), so T is 0.87 fy Ast.
  • Taking the larger root of the quadratic. The root above d (788 mm in the first example) is not a neutral axis inside the beam.
  • Leaving Mu in kN·m inside a formula in N and mm. 1 kN·m = 10⁶ N·mm.
  • Skipping the xu ≤ xu,max check in an analysis question. If xu comes out above xu,max, the moment of resistance is limited to Mu,lim, not the value from the couple.

2.2 Balanced, under-reinforced and over-reinforced sections

How a beam fails depends on how much tension steel it has. With little steel the bars yield first, the beam sags and cracks widely, and people get a warning. With too much steel the concrete crushes first, suddenly. Between the two, where both happen at once, is the balanced section. IS 456 fixes that boundary as xu,max and allows only sections on the ductile side of it.

You need the force and moment equations of singly reinforced beams and the design stress-strain curves of concrete and steel.

Strain diagram and the three modes

At collapse the top fibre is at the crushing strain εcu = 0.0035. Plane sections stay plane, so the strain in the tension steel follows from similar triangles:

εs = 0.0035 (d − xu) / xu

εs is the strain at the tension steel. A shallow neutral axis gives a large steel strain.

IS 456 calls the steel yielded when εs reaches εs,lim = 0.87 fy/Es + 0.002, with Es = 2 × 10⁵ N/mm². The extra 0.002 is where a cold-worked bar reaches 0.87 fy on its design curve. Setting εs = εs,lim gives the balanced depth:

xu,max / d = 0.0035 / (0.0055 + 0.87 fy/Es)

For Fe250: 0.0035/(0.0055 + 0.00109) = 0.531; Fe415: 0.0035/(0.0055 + 0.00181) = 0.479; Fe500: 0.0035/(0.0055 + 0.00218) = 0.456. IS 456 rounds these to 0.53, 0.48 and 0.46.

  • Under-reinforced: xu < xu,max. The steel reaches εs,lim before the concrete crushes; large deflection and wide cracks give warning. Mu = 0.87 fy Ast (d − 0.42 xu).
  • Balanced: xu = xu,max. Steel reaches εs,lim as the concrete crushes. The moment is Mu,lim.
  • Over-reinforced: xu > xu,max. The concrete crushes while the steel stress is still below 0.87 fy; failure is sudden. IS 456 does not allow it in design, and in an analysis question the moment of resistance is limited to Mu,lim.

Some texts call these tension-controlled and compression-controlled sections; the test is still xu against xu,max, or Ast against Ast,lim.

Limiting steel and limiting percentage

At the balanced point C = T with the steel at 0.87 fy:

Ast,lim = 0.36 fck b xu,max / (0.87 fy)     pt,lim = 100 Ast,lim/(b d) = 41.4 (fck/fy)(xu,max/d)

Ast,lim is the most tension steel a singly reinforced section may have, in mm²; pt,lim the same as a percentage of b d; 41.4 = 100 × 0.36/0.87.

Analysing an over-reinforced section

If 0.87 fy Ast/(0.36 fck b) gives xu above xu,max, that xu is wrong: it assumed the steel was at 0.87 fy, but it has not yielded. The true xu comes from strain compatibility. Guess xu, find εs from the strain diagram, read fs from the design curve of the bar, and compare C = 0.36 fck b xu with T = fs Ast. Raise xu if C < T, lower it if C > T, and repeat until they agree. Because fs < 0.87 fy, the true xu is smaller than the first estimate.

  1. Compute xu,max and Ast,lim for the grades.
  2. If Ast ≤ Ast,lim, the section is under- or just balanced: xu = 0.87 fy Ast/(0.36 fck b) and Mu = 0.87 fy Ast (d − 0.42 xu).
  3. If Ast > Ast,lim, it is over-reinforced. For a code answer, Mu = Mu,lim. To find the real collapse moment, solve xu by strain compatibility and take Mu = 0.36 fck b xu (d − 0.42 xu).

If more moment is needed, compression steel (doubly reinforced beams) raises the capacity and keeps the beam ductile. The design aids stop at pt,lim for the same reason.

Worked example

Question. A beam 300 mm wide with an effective depth of 500 mm is made of M30 concrete and Fe500 steel. Classify the section and find its moment of resistance when (a) Ast = 1500 mm² and (b) Ast = 2500 mm².

  1. xu,max = 0.46 × 500 = 230 mm. Ast,lim = 0.36 × 30 × 300 × 230/(0.87 × 500) = 745 200/435 = 1713.1 mm², so pt,lim = 100 × 1713.1/(300 × 500) = 1.142 %.
  2. Mu,lim = 0.36 × 30 × 300 × 230 × (500 − 0.42 × 230) = 745 200 × 403.4 = 300.6 × 10⁶ N·mm = 300.6 kN·m.
  3. (a) pt = 100 × 1500/150 000 = 1.000 % < 1.142 %: under-reinforced. xu = 0.87 × 500 × 1500/(0.36 × 30 × 300) = 652 500/3240 = 201.4 mm < 230 mm.
  4. Steel strain εs = 0.0035 × (500 − 201.4)/201.4 = 0.00519, above εs,lim = 0.87 × 500/200 000 + 0.002 = 0.00418, so the steel has yielded as assumed.
  5. Mu = 652 500 × (500 − 0.42 × 201.4) = 652 500 × 415.4 = 271.1 × 10⁶ N·mm = 271.1 kN·m.
  6. (b) pt = 100 × 2500/150 000 = 1.667 % > 1.142 %: over-reinforced. The code moment of resistance is Mu,lim = 300.6 kN·m.

Answer. (a) Under-reinforced, xu = 201.4 mm, Mu = 271.1 kN·m. (b) Over-reinforced; IS 456 credits only Mu,lim = 300.6 kN·m.

Worked example 2

Question. For case (b) above (Ast = 2500 mm²), find the true neutral axis depth and collapse moment by strain compatibility, using the IS 456 design curve for Fe500.

  1. First estimate with 0.87 fy: xu = 435 × 2500/3240 = 335.6 mm. It is above 230 mm, so the steel has not yielded and this value is not the answer.
  2. Trial xu = 300 mm: εs = 0.0035 × 200/300 = 0.00233, fs = 394.7 N/mm² from the curve; C = 0.36 × 30 × 300 × 300 = 972.0 kN, T = 394.7 × 2500 = 986.9 kN. C < T, so xu must grow.
  3. Trial xu = 310 mm: εs = 0.0035 × 190/310 = 0.00215, fs = 383.6 N/mm²; C = 1004.4 kN, T = 959.0 kN. C > T, so xu lies between 300 and 310 mm.
  4. Converged: xu = 302.8 mm, εs = 0.00228, fs = 392.4 N/mm²; C = T = 981.0 kN.
  5. Mu = 981.0 × 10³ × (500 − 0.42 × 302.8) = 981.0 × 10³ × 372.8 = 365.8 × 10⁶ N·mm = 365.8 kN·m.
  6. So 46 % more steel than Ast,lim raised the collapse moment by 22 %, while the steel strain at failure fell to 0.00228, below 0.00418.

Answer. xu = 302.8 mm (not 335.6 mm), fs = 392.4 N/mm², true collapse moment 365.8 kN·m; the code value stays Mu,lim = 300.6 kN·m because the failure is brittle.

Exam traps

  • Calling a beam under-reinforced because its steel looks small. 1500 mm² is under-reinforced in the 300 × 500 M30 beam above but would be over-reinforced in a 230 × 300 M20 beam (Ast,lim = 0.36 × 20 × 230 × 0.48 × 250/361.05 = 550 mm² for d = 250 mm).
  • Using xu,max/d = 0.48 for Fe500. The value is 0.46; with 0.48 the limiting moment of the example would be 310.4 kN·m instead of 300.6 kN·m.
  • Using 0.36 fck b xu = 0.87 fy Ast for an over-reinforced section. In example (b) it gives 335.6 mm against the true 302.8 mm, because the steel stress is only 392.4 N/mm², not 435 N/mm².
  • Taking the yield strain fy/Es as the balanced strain. IS 456 uses 0.87 fy/Es + 0.002; with fy/Es alone, xu,max/d for Fe415 would come out 0.63.
  • Comparing pt with a fixed value such as 1 % instead of pt,lim for the actual concrete and steel grades.

2.3 Doubly reinforced beams

A singly reinforced section stops at Mu,lim: more tension steel would only make it over-reinforced. When the moment is larger and the depth is fixed by headroom, steel is also placed near the compression face. That compression steel Asc, together with an equal force of extra tension steel Ast2, forms a second couple that carries the moment above Mu,lim while the concrete block stays at xu,max. Beams at supports of continuous frames and in earthquake frames are doubly reinforced.

You need singly reinforced beams and the limit xu,max from balanced and over-reinforced sections.

Two couples in one section

Part 1 is the singly reinforced beam at its limit: block at xu,max, steel Ast,lim, moment Mu,lim. Part 2 is a steel couple: compression steel Asc at depth d′ balanced by extra tension steel Ast2, with lever arm d − d′, carrying Mu − Mu,lim.

Mu − Mu,lim = fsc Asc (d − d′)     0.87 fy Ast2 = fsc Asc     Ast = Ast,lim + Ast2

fsc is the design stress in the compression steel (Annex G-1.2), in N/mm².

Some textbooks write the steel force as (fsc − 0.446 fck) Asc to subtract the displaced concrete; that raises Asc a few per cent. This book uses the Annex G form.

The stress in the compression steel

The compression steel strain is less than the 0.0035 at the top:

εsc = 0.0035 (xu,max − d′) / xu,max

fsc is then read from the design stress-strain curve of the bar.

For Fe250 (mild steel, elastic then flat) fsc = 0.87 fy = 217.5 N/mm² once εsc ≥ 0.87 fy/Es = 0.00109. Fe415 and Fe500 are cold-worked bars with a curved design diagram (IS 456 Fig. 23A): the stress is linear up to 0.8 × 0.87 fy and reaches 0.87 fy only at a strain of 0.87 fy/Es + 0.002. For Fe415 that strain is 0.0038, more than the compression steel ever gets, so fsc is below 361 N/mm². SP 16 Table F lists the result against d′/d:

Design and analysis procedures

  1. Design: compute Mu,lim. If Mu ≤ Mu,lim, design a singly reinforced section instead.
  2. Set xu = xu,max and find εsc = 0.0035(xu,max − d′)/xu,max; read fsc (or take it from Table F for d′/d).
  3. Asc = (Mu − Mu,lim)/[fsc (d − d′)] and Ast2 = fsc Asc/(0.87 fy).
  4. Ast = Ast,lim + Ast2. Check Ast and Asc are each at most 0.04 b D, choose bars, and tie the compression bars with stirrups so they cannot buckle.
  5. Analysis of a given section: assume the tension steel yields, guess xu, find εsc and fsc, and adjust xu until 0.36 fck b xu + fsc Asc = 0.87 fy Ast. Confirm xu ≤ xu,max, then Mu = 0.36 fck b xu (d − 0.42 xu) + fsc Asc (d − d′).

Compression steel also reduces long-term deflection (deflection control). When the excess moment is large, a deeper section or a flanged beam is often cheaper. The design aids tabulate pt and pc for doubly reinforced sections.

Worked example

Question. Design a beam 250 mm wide with an effective depth of 500 mm (d′ = 50 mm) for a factored moment of 250 kN·m, using M25 concrete and Fe415 steel.

  1. xu,max = 0.48 × 500 = 240 mm. Mu,lim = 0.36 × 25 × 250 × 240 × (500 − 0.42 × 240) = 540 000 × 399.2 = 215.57 kN·m.
  2. Mu = 250 kN·m > 215.57 kN·m, so the beam is doubly reinforced. Excess moment ΔM = 250 − 215.57 = 34.43 kN·m.
  3. εsc = 0.0035 × (240 − 50)/240 = 0.00277. On the Fe415 curve this lies just above (0.00276, 351.8), so fsc = 352.1 N/mm² (Table F, d′/d = 0.10: 353 N/mm²).
  4. Asc = 34.43 × 10⁶ / (352.1 × 450) = 217.3 mm².
  5. Ast2 = 352.1 × 217.3/(0.87 × 415) = 76 510/361.05 = 211.9 mm².
  6. Ast,lim = 0.36 × 25 × 250 × 240/361.05 = 540 000/361.05 = 1495.6 mm². Ast = 1495.6 + 211.9 = 1707.6 mm².
  7. Provide 2 bars of 25 mm + 2 bars of 22 mm at the bottom (982 + 760 = 1742 mm²) and 2 bars of 12 mm at the top (226 mm²). With D = 550 mm, 0.04 b D = 5500 mm², well above both.

Answer. Asc = 217 mm² (2 bars of 12 mm) and Ast = 1708 mm² (2 of 25 mm + 2 of 22 mm, 1742 mm²); fsc = 352 N/mm², not 361 N/mm².

Worked example 2

Question. A beam 300 mm wide has d = 550 mm and d′ = 50 mm, with 4 bars of 25 mm in tension and 2 bars of 20 mm in compression. M20 concrete, Fe415 steel. Find its moment of resistance.

  1. Ast = 1963.5 mm²; Asc = 628.3 mm². Tension, if the bars yield: T = 0.87 × 415 × 1963.5 = 708.9 kN.
  2. Trial xu = 220 mm: εsc = 0.0035 × 170/220 = 0.00270, fsc = 350.6 N/mm²; C = 0.36 × 20 × 300 × 220 + 350.6 × 628.3 = 475.2 + 220.3 = 695.5 kN < 708.9 kN. Increase xu.
  3. Trial xu = 230 mm: εsc = 0.00274, fsc = 351.5 N/mm²; C = 496.8 + 220.8 = 717.6 kN > 708.9 kN. So xu lies between.
  4. Converged: xu = 226.1 mm, fsc = 351.1 N/mm²; Cc = 488.3 kN, Cs = 220.6 kN, total 708.9 kN.
  5. Checks: xu,max = 0.48 × 550 = 264 mm, so xu < xu,max; tension steel strain = 0.0035 × (550 − 226.1)/226.1 = 0.0050, above 0.0038, so the bars yield as assumed.
  6. Mu = 488.3 × (550 − 0.42 × 226.1) + 220.6 × (550 − 50) = 488.3 × 455.0 + 220.6 × 500 = 222.2 + 110.3 = 332.5 kN·m (forces in kN, arms in m ÷ 1000).

Answer. xu = 226.1 mm, fsc = 351 N/mm², Mu = 332.5 kN·m; without the compression bars the same tension steel would make the beam over-reinforced (Mu,lim = 250.4 kN·m).

Exam traps

2.4 Flanged beams: T and L sections

In a building the slab and its beams are cast together, so near midspan the slab works as a wide compression flange on top of the beam. A beam with slab on both sides is a T-beam; an edge beam with slab on one side is an L-beam. The flange gives so much compression area that the neutral axis usually stays inside it. The design asks how wide the flange is, where the neutral axis lies and how much steel balances the compression.

You need the stress block and the moment equations of singly reinforced beams.

Effective flange width (IS 456 clause 23.1.2)

The slab is stressed most next to the web (shear lag), so the code uses a uniform stress over an effective width bf. bw is the web breadth, Df the slab thickness and L0 the distance between points of zero moment (the span of a simply supported beam, about 0.7 times the span of a continuous one).

BeamEffective flange width bf
T-beamL0/6 + bw + 6 Df
L-beamL0/12 + bw + 3 Df
Isolated T-beamL0/(L0/b + 4) + bw, with b the actual flange width
Isolated L-beam0.5 L0/(L0/b + 4) + bw

bf may not exceed the web plus half the clear distance to the next beam on each side. Over a continuous support the slab is in tension and the section is a rectangle bw wide.

Neutral axis in the flange

If Mu ≤ Mf = 0.36 fck bf Df (d − 0.42 Df), the neutral axis lies in the flange and the beam is designed as a rectangle of width bf:

Mu = 0.36 fck bf xu (d − 0.42 xu)     Ast = 0.36 fck bf xu / (0.87 fy)

For an analysis, compare T = 0.87 fy Ast with 0.36 fck bf Df: if T is smaller, xu = T/(0.36 fck bf) lies in the flange.

Most floor T-beams fall in this case; minimum steel is 0.85 bw d/fy.

Neutral axis in the web (IS 456 Annex G-2.2)

If xu > Df, split the compression into the web (width bw, usual block) and the overhangs (width bf − bw). The top 3/7 of the compression depth is on the flat part of the curve at 0.45 fck. If the slab lies within it (Df ≤ 3/7 xu) the overhangs carry 0.45 fck over their full depth; otherwise the code uses yf = 0.15 xu + 0.65 Df, not more than Df.

Cw = 0.36 fck bw xu     Cf = 0.45 fck (bf − bw) yf     0.87 fy Ast = Cw + Cf

yf = Df when Df ≤ 3/7 xu, otherwise yf = 0.15 xu + 0.65 Df ≤ Df. Cw acts 0.42 xu below the top, Cf at yf/2.

Mu = Cw (d − 0.42 xu) + Cf (d − yf/2)

With xu = xu,max this gives the limiting moment of the T-beam, Mu,lim,T. If Mu exceeds it, compression steel is added as for doubly reinforced beams.

  1. Find bf (formula and spacing limit, the smaller).
  2. Compare T or Mu with the flange alone at xu = Df.
  3. If not enough, solve Cw + Cf = T with yf = Df; if Df > 3/7 xu, solve again with yf = 0.15 xu + 0.65 Df.
  4. Check xu ≤ xu,max, find Mu, and check the deflection with kf.

2.5 Design aids for flexure

Design aids are tables and charts that solve the flexure equations once for every case, so a designer reads the steel instead of solving a quadratic. The Indian set is SP 16, Design Aids for Reinforced Concrete to IS 456. You enter a table with Mu/bd² and read the steel percentage pt. Only a few universities teach the aids as a topic, but many allow SP 16 in the exam hall, and using it correctly saves ten minutes on a design question.

You need the Annex G steel formula of singly reinforced beams.

What the tables contain

2.6 Serviceability: deflection and crack control

A beam that is safe against collapse can still sag enough to crack the partitions under it, or crack enough to let water reach its bars. The limit state of serviceability checks the beam under the loads that actually act, the service loads, with a load factor of 1.0. IS 456 controls deflection mainly by a span to depth rule and controls cracking mainly by detailing rules on bar spacing and cover, with a crack-width formula for cases that need a number.

You need the designed beams of singly reinforced beams; the cracked-section stresses behind the crack-width formula are in cracking moment and transformed sections.

Deflection limits (IS 456 clause 23.2)

Deflection grows with time: the immediate (short-term) sag under load is followed by creep of the concrete under the sustained load and by shrinkage as it dries, which together can double or triple it. IS 456 sets two limits for beams and slabs:

What is limitedLimit
Final deflection, including creep, shrinkage and temperature, measured from the as-cast levelspan/250
Deflection that happens after partitions and finishes are built, including creep and shrinkagespan/350 or 20 mm, whichever is less

L/350 equals 20 mm at a span of 7 m, so the 20 mm cap governs only for spans longer than 7 m.

The span to effective depth rule

For spans up to 10 m, a beam that satisfies the span/depth rule is taken to meet both limits without computing deflection:

L/d ≤ (L/d)basic × kt × kc × kf

(L/d)basic = 7 for a cantilever, 20 for a simply supported span and 26 for a continuous span; kt is the modification factor for tension steel (IS 456 Fig. 4), kc for compression steel (Fig. 5) and kf for flanged beams (Fig. 6). For spans over 10 m the basic value is multiplied by 10/span, except for cantilevers, which need a deflection calculation.

kt depends on the steel percentage pt and on the service stress in the steel, fs = 0.58 fy × (Ast required/Ast provided). It is largest, up to 2.0, for lightly reinforced beams: a low pt gives a shallow cracked neutral axis and a stiffer section for the same steel stress. It falls as pt or fs rises. Many designers use the curve fit kt = 1/[0.225 + 0.00322 fs + 0.625 log₁₀ pt] ≤ 2 in place of reading Fig. 4; in an exam, read the chart. kc grows from 1.0 with the compression steel percentage pc to 1.5 at pc = 3 % (close to kc = 1 + pc/(3 + pc)). For flanged beams kf is 0.8 when bw/bf ≤ 0.3 and rises linearly to 1.0 at bw/bf = 1, and pt is then worked out on bf d.

If the check fails, more depth helps most, because L/d and fs fall together; extra tension steel or compression steel also helps.

Crack control

Cracks in a beam cannot be avoided, because concrete cracks at a tensile stress of only 0.7√fck. The aim is many fine cracks instead of a few wide ones. IS 456 (clause 35.3.2) limits the surface crack width to 0.3 mm in general, 0.2 mm where the member is exposed to weather, moisture or soil, and 0.1 mm in a severe environment. In most beams the limit is met by detailing alone:

  • The clear distance between tension bars may not exceed the values of Table 15: 300 mm for Fe250, 180 mm for Fe415 and 150 mm for Fe500 (no moment redistribution).
  • Beams deeper than 750 mm get side face steel of 0.1 % of the web area, split between the two faces at not more than 300 mm or the web thickness.
  • Nominal cover by exposure (Table 16): 20 mm mild, 30 mm moderate, 45 mm severe, 50 mm very severe, 75 mm extreme.
  • Several smaller bars control cracks better than a few large bars of the same area.
wcr = 3 acr εm / [1 + 2 (acr − Cmin)/(D − x)]     εm = ε1 − b (D − x)(a − x) / [3 Es Ast (d − x)]

IS 456 Annex F. acr is the distance from the point considered to the surface of the nearest bar; Cmin the clear cover to the main bars; x the depth of the cracked neutral axis; a the depth of the point considered (a = D at the soffit); ε1 the strain there from the cracked section; the second term of εm is the stiffening of the concrete between cracks.

2.7 Cracking moment and transformed sections

Below its cracking moment a reinforced beam behaves as one elastic material, and above it the concrete under the neutral axis cracks and drops out. Both states are analysed with a transformed section: the steel is replaced by an equivalent area of concrete, m times larger, where m = Es/Ec is the modular ratio. The uncracked transformed section gives the cracking moment Mcr; the cracked one gives the service stresses and the stiffness used for crack widths and deflections. Only a few syllabi list this as a separate topic, but every deflection and crack check rests on it.

You need the elastic properties of concrete and steel, and the serviceability checks that use these results.

Modular ratio and the uncracked section

Steel and concrete at the same level have the same strain, so the steel stress is m times the concrete stress there. With Es = 2 × 10⁵ N/mm² and the short-term modulus Ec = 5000√fck (IS 456 clause 6.2.3.1), M25 gives Ec = 25 000 N/mm² and m = 8. For the working stress method and for service-load stresses IS 456 uses m = 280/(3σcbc) instead (10.98 for M25, 13.33 for M20), which allows for creep under sustained load.

Before cracking, the whole concrete section b × D acts, and the steel adds (m − 1)Ast: m Ast carries the steel's force, minus the Ast of concrete the bars already displace. The neutral axis ȳ (from the top) and the second moment of area I follow by taking moments of areas:

ȳ = [b D²/2 + (m − 1) Ast d] / [b D + (m − 1) Ast]     I = b D³/12 + b D (ȳ − D/2)² + (m − 1) Ast (d − ȳ)²

b, D and d in mm; Ast in mm²; I in mm⁴.

Cracking moment

The first flexural crack opens when the bottom fibre reaches the flexural tensile strength fcr = 0.7√fck (IS 456 clause 6.2.2). With yt = D − ȳ the distance from the neutral axis to that fibre:

Mcr = fcr I / yt     (IS 456 Annex C: Mr = fcr Igr / yt with the gross section, ignoring the steel)

Mcr in N·mm. The transformed I gives a value about 15 % higher; the code's gross-section value is the one used in the deflection formula below.

In a normally designed beam Mcr is well below the service moment, so at service load the beam is cracked over most of its span. That is why service stresses come from the cracked section.

The cracked transformed section

Above Mcr the concrete below the neutral axis is ignored and the steel becomes m Ast. Moments of area about the cracked neutral axis, depth x, give a quadratic; then the cracked second moment Icr and the stresses follow:

b x²/2 = m Ast (d − x)     Icr = b x³/3 + m Ast (d − x)²     fc = M x/Icr     fs = m M (d − x)/Icr

fc is the concrete stress at the top fibre and fs the steel stress, both in N/mm², under the service moment M in N·mm. The lever arm is z = d − x/3.

The real stiffness lies between Icr and Igr, because the concrete between cracks still carries some tension. IS 456 Annex C gives an effective moment of inertia for short-term deflection:

Ieff = Icr / [1.2 − (Mr/M)(z/d)(1 − x/d)(bw/b)],   Icr ≤ Ieff ≤ Igr

M is the largest service moment; bw/b = 1 for a rectangle. The short-term deflection of a simply supported beam under a uniform load w is then 5wL⁴/(384 Ec Ieff).

Worked example

Question. A beam has b = 300 mm, D = 500 mm, d = 450 mm and Ast = 1200 mm², in M25 concrete. Using Ec = 5000√fck, find m, the uncracked transformed section and the cracking moment, and compare it with the Annex C gross-section value.

  1. Ec = 5000√25 = 25 000 N/mm²; m = 200 000/25 000 = 8.00. Added area (m − 1)Ast = 7 × 1200 = 8400 mm².
  2. ȳ = (300 × 500 × 250 + 8400 × 450)/(150 000 + 8400) = (37 500 000 + 3 780 000)/158 400 = 260.6 mm.
  3. I = 300 × 500³/12 + 150 000 × 10.6² + 8400 × 189.4² = 3125 × 10⁶ + 16.9 × 10⁶ + 301.3 × 10⁶ = 3443 × 10⁶ mm⁴.
  4. fcr = 0.7√25 = 3.5 N/mm²; yt = 500 − 260.6 = 239.4 mm.
  5. Mcr = 3.5 × 3443 × 10⁶/239.4 = 50.3 × 10⁶ N·mm = 50.3 kN·m.
  6. Annex C: Mr = 3.5 × (300 × 500³/12)/250 = 3.5 × 3125 × 10⁶/250 = 43.75 kN·m. The steel raises the cracking moment by 15 %.

Answer. m = 8; ȳ = 260.6 mm, I = 3443 × 10⁶ mm⁴; Mcr = 50.3 kN·m (gross section, as Annex C uses: 43.75 kN·m).

Worked example 2

Question. The same beam spans 6 m, simply supported, and carries a service moment of 60 kN·m. With m = 8, find the cracked neutral axis, the concrete and steel stresses, Ieff and the short-term midspan deflection.

  1. 60 kN·m > Mr = 43.75 kN·m, so the beam is cracked.
  2. 300 x²/2 = 8 × 1200 × (450 − x), that is 150 x² + 9600 x − 4 320 000 = 0, so x = 140.7 mm.
  3. Icr = 300 × 140.7³/3 + 9600 × 309.3² = 278.5 × 10⁶ + 918.4 × 10⁶ = 1197 × 10⁶ mm⁴, only 38 % of Igr = 3125 × 10⁶ mm⁴.
  4. fc = 60 × 10⁶ × 140.7/(1197 × 10⁶) = 7.05 N/mm²; fs = 8 × 60 × 10⁶ × 309.3/(1197 × 10⁶) = 124.0 N/mm² (check: M/(Ast z) = 60 × 10⁶/(1200 × 403.1) = 124.0).
  5. Ieff = 1197 × 10⁶/[1.2 − (43.75/60)(403.1/450)(1 − 140.7/450)] = 1197 × 10⁶/(1.2 − 0.449) = 1594 × 10⁶ mm⁴, between Icr and Igr.
  6. Service load w = 8M/L² = 8 × 60/6² = 13.33 kN/m = 13.33 N/mm. Deflection = 5 × 13.33 × 6000⁴/(384 × 25 000 × 1594 × 10⁶) = 5.6 mm (with Igr it would be 2.9 mm, with Icr 7.5 mm).

Answer. x = 140.7 mm, fc = 7.05 N/mm², fs = 124.0 N/mm², Ieff = 1594 × 10⁶ mm⁴; short-term deflection 5.6 mm, well inside 6000/250 = 24 mm before creep and shrinkage are added.

Exam traps

  • Using m Ast in the uncracked section. The bars displace concrete, so the added area is (m − 1)Ast; m Ast is right only in the cracked section, where the concrete around the bars is ignored.
  • Using Icr to compute Mcr. Mcr belongs to the uncracked section; with Icr = 1197 × 10⁶ mm⁴ the example would give a meaningless 'Mcr' below 20 kN·m.
  • Taking yt = D/2 for the transformed section. The steel moves the axis down to 260.6 mm, so yt = 239.4 mm, not 250 mm.
  • Comparing Mcr with the factored moment Mu. Mcr is a service-load quantity; compare it with the service moment.
  • Mixing the two modular ratios: Es/Ec (8 for M25) for short-term elastic behaviour, 280/(3σcbc) (10.98 for M25) for the working stress method and service stresses. State which one you use.

Beyond the syllabus

Not for the exam. Prestressed concrete is designed so that the section stays uncracked at service load, and then the uncracked transformed section of this chapter is the working tool. For long-term behaviour designers use an age-adjusted modular ratio, Es/[Ec/(1 + χφ)], with the creep coefficient φ. Search for age-adjusted effective modulus and tension stiffening.

Takeaways

  1. At collapse a beam is a couple: the concrete block 0.36 fck b xu at 0.42 xu from the top, and the steel 0.87 fy Ast; C = T gives xu and the couple gives Mu.
  2. The neutral axis may not go below xu,max (0.53d, 0.48d, 0.46d), so a singly reinforced section stops at Mu,lim = 0.148, 0.138 or 0.133 fck b d².
  3. A section with Ast above Ast,lim is over-reinforced: it crushes suddenly, and the code credits only Mu,lim however much steel it has.
  4. Above Mu,lim, compression steel and an equal force of extra tension steel carry the excess; for Fe415 and Fe500 the compression steel stress comes from the curved design diagram (about 353 N/mm² for Fe415), not 0.87 fy.
  5. A monolithic slab makes a T or L beam: find bf from the code, test whether the flange can balance the steel, and use 0.45 fck on the overhangs with yf when the neutral axis is in the web.
  6. SP 16 tables are the same equations solved in advance: enter with Mu/bd², read pt (and pc for doubly reinforced sections).
  7. Serviceability is checked at service loads: L/d against 20 × kt × kc × kf for deflection, bar spacing and cover for cracks, and the Annex F formula when a crack width is needed.
  8. The transformed section links the two states: (m − 1)Ast before cracking gives Mcr, m Ast after cracking gives the service stresses and the stiffness for deflection.

  8. Recommendation: scheme C, the T-beam, with 3 bars of 28 mm. It is valid only if the slab is cast monolithically with the beam and the beam is simply supported (sagging moment over the whole span); for a continuous beam the support sections would be rectangles 300 mm wide and would need their own check, where scheme A or B governs.
  9. Check: in every scheme the steel provided gives at least 300 kN·m (A by the two-couple design, B: 361.05 × 1963.5 × (550 − 110.3) = 311.7 kN·m, C: 361.05 × 1847 × (500 − 0.42 × 49.4) = 319.6 kN·m).

Glossary

Balanced section
A section in which the steel reaches its limiting strain as the concrete reaches 0.0035.
Cold-worked bar
A high-strength deformed bar (Fe415, Fe500) whose design stress-strain curve bends gradually instead of yielding sharply.
Compression steel
Bars near the compression face that carry part of the compression in a doubly reinforced beam.
Cracking moment
The moment at which the extreme tension fibre reaches the flexural tensile strength.
Creep
The slow growth of strain in concrete under a sustained stress.
Effective cover d′
Distance from the compression face to the centroid of the compression steel.
Effective depth d
Distance from the extreme compression fibre to the centroid of the tension steel.
Effective flange width
The width of slab assumed to act with a T or L beam at a uniform stress.
Effective moment of inertia
Ieff, a stiffness between Icr and Igr that allows for concrete between cracks.
Lever arm
The distance z between the compression and tension resultants, d − 0.42 xu for a rectangular section.
Limiting moment of resistance
Mu,lim, the largest moment a singly reinforced section may carry.
Limiting neutral axis depth
xu,max, the deepest neutral axis that still lets the steel reach its limiting strain.
Modification factor
A factor (kt, kc, kf) that raises or lowers the basic span/depth ratio.
Modular ratio
m, the ratio of the elastic modulus of steel to that of concrete.
Neutral axis
The level of zero strain in a bent section; its depth from the top is xu.
Over-reinforced section
A section with xu above xu,max; the concrete crushes first and the failure is sudden.
Points of contraflexure
Points where the bending moment changes sign; their spacing L0 sets the flange width of a continuous beam.
Service load
The characteristic load with a load factor of 1.0, used for serviceability checks.
Shear lag
The fall of compressive stress in a flange with distance from the web.
Shrinkage
The shortening of concrete as it dries, which also bends an unsymmetrically reinforced beam.
Side face reinforcement
Longitudinal bars on the sides of beams deeper than 750 mm to control cracks there.
SP 16
The Indian design aids for reinforced concrete to IS 456: tables and charts for flexure, compression and other actions.
Stress block
The idealised shape of concrete stress at collapse, replaced by a force 0.36 fck b xu at 0.42 xu.
Tension stiffening
The stiffness added by uncracked concrete between flexural cracks.
Transformed section
A section in which steel is replaced by an equivalent area of concrete so one elastic analysis applies.
Under-reinforced section
A section with xu below xu,max; the steel yields first and the beam fails with warning.
